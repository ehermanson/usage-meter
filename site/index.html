Questions

Why would I want this?

Most coding tools bury your rate-limit usage behind a menu or a command, and if you use more than one, you're checking each separately. Usage Meter keeps every provider's current usage and reset time in your menu bar. The ring runs green to amber to red as a window fills, and the bars in the dropdown turn yellow at 75% and red at 90%, so you can pace your work or switch tools before you hit the wall. Each window also says whether you're ahead of pace, on pace, or have room to spare, with a marker on the bar where even usage would be, and when you're ahead, how long until you'd hit the limit.

Which tools does it track?

Claude Code, Codex, and Gemini (via Antigravity or the Gemini CLI). For each one it shows the rate-limit windows (5-hour and weekly, or daily for Gemini) as percent used, with the time until they reset. When Codex grants you free rate-limit resets, it shows how many you have and lets you redeem one right from the dropdown. For Claude Code and Codex, the Tokens and Cost tabs also add up how much you've used over time.

What does the menu-bar item show?

The tool's own mark inside a ring that fills as its 5-hour window is used, then the numbers beside it. It follows whichever provider is closest to its limit unless you pin one, and you can trim it down to the 5-hour figure, to a bare ring, or spread it out to one ring per provider. The full picture is always a click away in the dropdown.

What do the Tokens and Cost tabs show?

A look back, rather than at the limits. Tokens adds up what Claude Code and Codex processed over the last 24 hours or 7, 30, or 90 days, charted with a line for each; Cost puts an API-equivalent price on the same tokens. Both are read from the session logs those two tools already keep on your Mac, so Gemini shows up under Limits only. Cost is an estimate at API list prices, not a bill: subscription plans aren't billed per token, so read it as a measure of how much you use, not what you pay.

Do I need API keys or an account?

No. Usage Meter reads the sign-ins those tools already keep on your Mac. There's nothing to paste and no account to create.

Where does my usage data go?

It stays between your Mac and each provider. Your limits come from the provider itself, asked for with the sign-in its own tool already keeps, and the Tokens and Cost figures are worked out on your Mac from Claude Code's and Codex's session logs. The app's only other requests go to GitHub, and none of them carries your usage: a check for new releases (plus the update itself, if you choose to install one) and, at most once a day, LiteLLM's public model price table for the cost estimate. No analytics, no telemetry.

Does this support other tools, like Grok or Cursor?

Not right now. It's open source, though, and a new provider is mostly one small client that reads that tool's usage. If you want one added, open an issue or send a pull request on GitHub. Contributions are welcome.

Is it free? What do I need?

Yes, free and open source under the MIT license. It runs on macOS 14 and later.
